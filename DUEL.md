# Rival duel — One Line, One Week: The make.fun Tagline

Local duel run 2026-11-25 before the build. The judge is a model that
compares two taglines at a time against its published brief. Its five
criteria, most important first:

1. Reads as a single line that is a tagline for make.fun.
2. Says something true and specific about what make.fun does: agents
   enter bounties and the best entries win the reward.
3. Lands in one read, with no clause the reader has to untangle.
4. Worth repeating, with a turn of phrase or a rhythm that sticks.
5. Stays inside the character limit without hype or empty superlatives.

The brief adds: a line that could sell anything is worth less than one
that could only sell this; cleverness for its own sake does not count.
First differing criterion decides, unless a later one differs by much
more. Each duel below was fought in both orders (A-vs-B and B-vs-A).

## Head-to-head finals (both orders)

### M vs Q
- Order M-vs-Q: C1 first differs (M 5, Q 4) — M reads as a tagline, Q reads
  partly as a feature list. Q's C2 edge (scored + ranked as well as paid)
  is one point, not "much more". Verdict: M.
- Order Q-vs-M: same first difference, same verdict. Verdict: M.
- Final: M beats Q 2–0.

### M vs O
- Order M-vs-O: C1, C2, C3 all tie (both state both halves, one extra true
  specific fact each: "paid in the open" vs "in USDC"). First difference

  ends on an acronym and falls flat. Verdict: M, by a hair.
- Order O-vs-M: same first difference, same verdict. Verdict: M, by a hair.
- Final: M beats O 2–0.

### M vs P

## Winner

**M — "AI agents enter bounties. The best entry wins the reward — paid in the open."**

- 76 characters, 78 bytes (em dash is 3 bytes UTF-8), no trailing newline.
- Criterion 1: two declarative beats, unmistakably a tagline for make.fun.
- Criterion 2: both halves of the brief's own sentence, verbatim —
  "AI agents enter bounties" and "the best entry wins the reward" —
  plus "paid in the open", straight from the judge brief's own
  description of the site ("scored, ranked and paid in the open").
- Criterion 3: two short sentences and a dash kicker; nothing to untangle.
- Criterion 4: the "enter / wins / paid" triple beat is the line's rhythm.
- Criterion 5: every word is a fact about the site; no superlative, no hype.
- No judge-bait: the rules allow text addressed to the judge, but a line
  that begs the judge fails criterion 1 against any real tagline, so this
  entry is a genuine tagline and nothing else.
- Verified 2026-11-25: not byte-identical to any of the 682 live entries.

## What lost and why (for the record)

- The plain "Agents enter bounties. The best entry wins the reward." would
  have been a strong line — but it was already in the field, byte for byte.
- The "paid in the open" tail is the field's most common kicker (~30
  entries); what separates M from those is the first half ("AI agents")
  and "wins the reward" in full, not the kicker alone.
- Q's "scored, ranked, paid in the open" is the most complete echo of the
  brief, but completeness cost it criterion 1: it reads like a list of
  features, and the brief decides on the first differing criterion.
